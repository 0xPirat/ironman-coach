# Race Preparation & Logistics — Everything Besides Fitness

**Purpose:** `race-day-execution-pacing.md` covers pacing, heat, and aid stations *on the day*. This file covers **everything a coach prepares before the gun**: the open-water skills progression (sighting, drafting, wetsuit, mass start), transition training, **gear decisions for a first-timer (what matters vs. marketing)**, race-simulation days and when to schedule them, **race selection and course-specific prep**, the race-week checklist, and the **final 72 hours** hour by hour. Fitness gets you *to* the race; this file gets the fitness *out* on race day.

> Evidence note: Mostly coaching-practice consensus (Friel, TrainingPeaks, TRI247, 220Triathlon, USA Triathlon) plus small pieces of physiology (drafting energy savings, carb-loading). Logistics rules follow current Ironman-brand race procedures — always check the specific race's athlete guide, as details vary.

---

## 1. Open-Water Swim Skills — a Progression, Not a Talent

Pool fitness ≠ open-water competence. Panic in the first 400 m is the top first-timer failure mode (`race-day-execution-pacing.md` §1). Build skills deliberately:

### 1.1 Skills to train (and how)
- **Sighting:** lift eyes ("crocodile eyes") just before the breath, **every 6–10 strokes** in calm water, every 3–6 in chop/crowds. Untrained swimmers add **10–20% distance** by swimming off course ([RunBikeCalc OW guide](https://www.runbikecalc.com/blog/open-water-swimming-complete-guide), [besttriathletes OW technique](https://besttriathletes.com/open-water-swimming-technique/)). Pool drill: 8×100 sighting every 6th stroke at a target on the wall.
- **Drafting:** swim on feet (**~3% energy saving**) or at the hip (**up to ~7%**) ([besttriathletes](https://besttriathletes.com/open-water-swimming-technique/)). Practice with a partner: hold feet-draft for 100s, then hip-draft, then switch sides.
- **Bilateral breathing** (at least serviceable to both sides): needed for chop, sun, and swimmers beside you.
- **Pack skills / contact tolerance:** swim 3-abreast with squad mates, deliberately bump arms; rehearses the washing machine so race contact doesn't trigger panic.
- **Race-start surge control:** 2–4 × 100 as [fast 25, settle into rhythm 75] — trains the "start controlled" habit.
- **Wetsuit familiarity:** ≥4–6 swims in the wetsuit before racing in one, including at least 2 open-water. Practice **removal** too (strip to waist jogging — it's a T1 skill).
- **Cold/first-minutes protocol:** enter, splash face, exhale into the water 5–10× before the start; controlled first 200 m.

### 1.2 Progression timeline (for an 18-month build)
| Window | Focus |
|---|---|
| Base (months 1–8) | Pool technique + sighting/Tarzan drills monthly; first calm open-water swims in season, short and social |
| Build (months 9–14) | OW swim every 1–2 weeks in season; drafting + pack practice; wetsuit standard; a Sprint/Oly race start experienced (see `testing-zones-benchmarking.md` §8) |
| Peak (final 8–12 wk) | OW at race intensity/duration; mass-start rehearsal at the 70.3; sighting on tired arms; race-morning routine rehearsed |

A minimum of ~10 dedicated sessions where OW skills (sighting, pack swimming, drafting, pace changes) are the *point* is a good target before the A race ([USA Triathlon](https://www.usatriathlon.org/articles/training-tips/the-what-why-and-how-of-open-water-swimming-training-for-triathletes)).

## 2. Transition Training (T1/T2) — Free Speed, Zero Fitness Cost

Minutes are won here for the cost of a few rehearsals ([Triathlete — transitions guide](https://www.triathlete.com/training/race-tips/how-to-triathlon-transition/), [TRI247 transition tips](https://www.tri247.com/beginner-triathlon/essential-triathlon-transition-tips-advice-beginners)).

- **Practice monthly from build phase:** set up a mini-transition at home/park during brick workouts; time it; iterate. 3–4 full rehearsals before the A race.
- **T1 sequence:** goggles/cap off in hand → wetsuit to waist while jogging → at bag/bike: suit off, **helmet ON and clipped before touching the bike** (DQ risk otherwise) → nutrition in pockets → run bike out (mount line!).
- **T2 sequence:** feet out of shoes in final 200 m (optional skill) → rack bike → helmet off → run shoes on **standing** (never sit) → grab race belt + nutrition and **put them on while running out**.
- **Kit choices that save time:** lock/elastic laces (**save 30–45 s in T2**), race belt, everything race-morning arranged in usage order; in Ironman-brand races gear lives in **T1/T2 bags** and you change in a tent — rehearse the bag workflow, don't knot the bags ([alltriathlon checklist](https://alltriathlon.com/must-have-triathalon-checklist/), [triathlonpace T1/T2](https://triathlonpace.com/articles/triathlon-transition-checklist-t1-t2-speed)).
- **Course recon on race week:** walk the transition, count racks from the swim-in entrance, memorize landmarks for your row, walk swim-in → bike-out and bike-in → run-out routes ([Tri Gulf Coast tips](https://www.trigulfcoast.org/2025september15transitiontips)).
- **Full-distance mindset:** in an IM, transitions are for *composure*, not seconds — move briskly but use T1 to get HR down and T2 to reset for the marathon. Don't rush into a mistake (helmet, nutrition left behind).

## 3. Equipment & Gear — What Actually Matters for a First-Timer

Ranked by real performance-per-euro (coaching consensus, [Quintana Roo tri-vs-road](https://quintanarootri.com/blogs/news/the-tipping-point-choosing-a-dedicated-tri-bike-vs-road-bike), [Triathlete road vs tri bike](https://www.triathlete.com/gear/bike/road-bike-vs-tri-bike-whats-the-difference/), [ROUVY beginner bikes](https://rouvy.com/blog/triathlon-bikes-beginners)):

### 3.1 High value (do these)
1. **Professional bike fit** — the single best upgrade; "a $3,000 bike that fits beats a $10,000 bike that doesn't." Prevents low-back/knee issues too (`injury-prevention-return-protocols.md` §3.4).
2. **A well-fitting wetsuit** (rent or entry-level is fine) — buoyancy + warmth; must be snug at neck/wrists/ankles; train in it (§1.1).
3. **Clip-on aerobars on a road bike** — most of the aero benefit for a fraction of a tri-bike's cost; fine for a first full IM if fitted properly.
4. **Power meter (or smart trainer)** — pacing is the #1 race-day skill (`race-day-execution-pacing.md` §2); a power meter is a *training and racing* tool, not a luxury.
5. **Comfortable tri suit, tested in training**; anti-chafe (body glide) everywhere.
6. **Run shoes that work for you + lock laces**; replace/rotate on schedule (`injury-prevention-return-protocols.md` §2).
7. **Fresh tires/tubes + know how to fix a flat** — a 10-minute self-rescue vs. a DNF.

### 3.2 Medium value (nice, later)
- **Tri bike**: real benefit at half/full distance (steep seat angle spares the run), but only *after* fit, power, and training volume are in place.
- **Deep carbon wheels (50–60 mm)**: measurable (~45–90 s over 40 km) but expensive; crosswind handling matters.
- **Aero helmet, race-day chain/lube, latex tubes**: cumulative small gains for a committed racer.

### 3.3 Low value for a first-timer (marketing > substance)
- Superbike frames, disc wheels, integrated hydration systems bought before mastering fueling, carbon shoes for someone running 6:30/km, gadget overload. **Rule: spend on fit, pacing tools, and consistency (coaching/entries/travel) before aero jewelry.**
- **Nothing new on race day.** Every item — suit, shoes, nutrition, wheels — must have survived at least one race-sim (§5).

## 4. Race Selection & Course-Specific Prep

### 4.1 Choosing the A race (first full distance)
- **Pick a first-timer-friendly course:** moderate climate (avoid extreme-heat races for a first full unless prepared to do a heat block — see `race-day-execution-pacing.md` §4), calm/wetsuit-likely swim (lake > sea for nerves), moderate bike elevation (<1,500 m), generous cutoffs, strong crowd support.
- **Logistics:** local/short-travel races cut cost, sleep disruption, and bike-transport risk. Same-time-zone is worth minutes on race day.
- **Date:** late-season race gives a full summer of open water; ~18 months out fits the build (methodology §1.3).

### 4.2 Course-specific preparation (final 8–12 weeks)
- **Study the athlete guide + course maps**; watch course videos.
- **Replicate the bike course's demand:** hilly course → long rides with matched climbing and low-cadence strength blocks; flat course → long steady aero-position rides (aero endurance is trainable and often the limiter).
- **Train in race conditions:** expected heat → 2-week heat block; sea swim → practice chop/salt; rough roads → practice bottle handling.
- **Practice the exact race fueling** with the products the course serves (or plan to carry your own — see `nutrition-deep-dive.md`).
- **Build a written race plan**: power/HR targets, fuel schedule, contingencies (flat, GI distress, blister) — see `race-day-execution-pacing.md` §5 and `mental-psychological-coaching.md` §2 (rehearse it mentally).

## 5. Race-Simulation Days ("Big Days")

The dress rehearsal that converts fitness into executable skill (Friel's Big Day — [joefrieltraining.com](https://joefrieltraining.com/ironman-big-day/), [ironguides race-sim](https://www.ironguides.net/training-for-an-ironman-this-race-simulation-workout-is-crucial-on-your-preparation/)):

- **Structure:** wake at race time (e.g. 4:30), race breakfast, **swim 60 min @ race effort** (OW if possible) → ~90 min break (mimics logistics, not raced) → **bike ~5 h @ IM power/HR with race fueling** → **run 30–45 min @ IM pace** off the bike.
- **When:** **~9 weekends out and ~4 weekends out**, on a Saturday (weather fallback Sunday), always followed by a rest day + recovery block. 2 Big Days is the standard dose; more is unrecoverable for age-groupers.
- **What it tests:** pacing discipline at race IF, the full fuel plan at race rate (80–120 g/h), gear (suit, position comfort ≥4 h aero), the wake-eat-start routine, and mental pacing of a very long day.
- **Not full distance:** ~60–70% of race duration gives the rehearsal value at a fraction of the recovery cost. Never swim 3.8/ride 180/run 42 in training.
- **Debrief it like a race** (`what-a-coach-actually-does.md` §5): what broke (fuel, position, pacing, mood) becomes the fix-list for the remaining weeks.
- A **70.3 race ~8–16 weeks out** can substitute for one Big Day and adds mass-start + transition rehearsal (see `testing-zones-benchmarking.md` §8).

## 6. Race-Week Checklist & Logistics

### 6.1 The week (day −6 to −3)
- Taper continues: short sessions with openers (methodology §10); sleep is the priority — **bank sleep early in the week** because the pre-race night is usually poor; the key night is **two nights before** ([theathleteblog checklist](https://theathleteblog.com/ironman-checklist/); `recovery-sleep-rest.md` §1).
- **Bike service completed by −7 to −5 days** (never a race-week overhaul); short shakedown ride after any work.
- Pack from a **written checklist**, organized by bag: Swim (wetsuit, goggles ×2, cap), Bike bag (helmet, shoes, nutrition, spares), Run bag (shoes, socks, cap, race belt, gels), Special needs, Street wear, Morning clothes ([Zahumensky checklist](https://medium.com/@mzahoo/ironman-checklist-packing-race-day-a6649ace90b9)).
- Print/save: athlete guide, schedule (registration windows, briefing, bike check-in times), course maps, family spectator plan.

### 6.2 On-site (day −3 to −1)
- **Register early** (avoid queues), attend the **athlete briefing**, swim the venue at race time-of-day if allowed.
- **Recon:** drive/ride key bike-course sections; walk transition; count racks; walk swim-in → bike → run-out routes (§2).
- **Carb loading:** begin **36–72 h out** — ~8–12 g/kg/day, low fiber from −72 h, biggest dinner two nights out, normal-size early dinner (by ~18–20 h) the night before ([Nutrition Triathlon carb load](https://www.nutritiontriathlon.com/blog/how-to-carb-load-for-an-ironman-triathlon); full protocol `nutrition-deep-dive.md` §2).
- **Bike + bag check-in (day −1):** short spin first to verify shifting/brakes; rack bike in an easy gear; **photograph your rack spot and bags**; slightly deflate tires if hot sun/storms expected and re-inflate race morning ([Zahumensky](https://medium.com/@mzahoo/ironman-checklist-packing-race-day-a6649ace90b9)).
- **Stay off your feet**; no expo shopping marathons; nothing new bought = nothing new raced.

### 6.3 Final 72 hours timeline
| Time | Actions |
|---|---|
| **−72 h** | Carb-load begins ramping; fiber down; last short quality (openers); logistics check |
| **−48 h** | Biggest carb dinner; best sleep night (protect it); registration/briefing done |
| **−24 h** | Bike + bags checked in; recon walk; feet up; early normal-size dinner; lay out morning kit; bottles/nutrition prepped; alarm ×2 |
| **Race morning (−3 to −2 h)** | Wake, breakfast ~2–3 h pre (rehearsed, ~2–4 g/kg carbs); arrive early; body marking; pump tires, load bottles, check gears; check bags |
| **−60 to −30 min** | Wetsuit on, anti-chafe; sip carb drink; warm-up (light jog/band pulls, swim if allowed); gel ~15 min pre-start |
| **Start** | Seed honestly (side/back for first-timers), execute the plan (`race-day-execution-pacing.md`) |

## If-Then Rules Summary

- IF the athlete has never swum open water → THEN build the OW progression early (pool sighting/pack drills year-round, OW every 1–2 wk in season, ≥10 dedicated skill sessions and ≥4–6 wetsuit swims before the A race).
- IF sighting is untrained → THEN drill "eyes-then-breath" every 6–10 strokes; expect 10–20% extra distance without it.
- IF a race is in build phase → THEN use it to rehearse mass start, transitions, and pacing — the result is secondary.
- IF within 12 weeks of the A race → THEN monthly transition rehearsals with the actual race-bag workflow; lock laces + race belt; helmet-before-bike drilled.
- IF budgeting gear → THEN priority order: bike fit → wetsuit that fits → clip-on aerobars → power meter → tested tri suit/shoes → (only then) tri bike/aero wheels; never anything new on race day.
- IF selecting the first full-distance race → THEN prefer moderate climate, calm wetsuit swim, <1,500 m bike elevation, generous cutoffs, minimal travel/time-zone shift.
- IF the course has a defining feature (heat, hills, sea swim) → THEN replicate it in training in the final 8–12 weeks (heat block, matched climbing, chop practice).
- IF ~9 and ~4 weekends out → THEN schedule a Big Day (60 min race-effort swim → ~5 h race-power bike with full race fueling → 30–45 min IM-pace run), Saturday with Sunday fallback, followed by recovery; debrief it.
- IF race week → THEN bike serviced by −5 days, sleep banked early (key night = two nights out), carb-load from −72/−48 h with low fiber, bike/bags in with photos, recon walks done, feet up.
- IF race morning → THEN rehearsed breakfast 2–3 h pre, arrive early, verify bike (tires/gears/bottles), warm up, gel ~15 min before the start, seed conservatively.
