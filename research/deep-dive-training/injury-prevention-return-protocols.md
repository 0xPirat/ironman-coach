# Injury Prevention & Return Protocols — Keeping the Athlete on the Road

**Purpose:** The methodology file states the *load rules* (≤10%/wk run, ramp-rate caps, HRV/readiness — `ironman-training-methodology.md` §3, §7–§8) and `recovery-sleep-rest.md` covers recovery modalities and burnout. This file is the **injury and illness manual**: the specific triathlon injuries, the **early warning signs** a coach watches, **prevention/prehab protocols with concrete exercises**, **pain decision rules** (train / modify / stop), and **structured return-to-training progressions after injury and after illness** (cold/flu/COVID). For a first-timer, *staying uninjured* is the single biggest predictor of getting to the start line — most DNS/DNF among age-groupers is injury-driven, and running carries the highest risk.

> Evidence note: Rehab exercise protocols (Alfredson eccentrics, glute-med work, etc.) have good clinical evidence. Pain-scale return rules and neck-check are well-established physio/sports-med convention. COVID return protocols follow published consensus statements. Nothing here replaces a physician/physio for a real injury — the AI coach's job is triage, load management, and adherence, not diagnosis.

---

## 1. The Big Triathlon Injuries (and their root cause)

Most are **overuse**, caused by load rising faster than tissue can adapt, plus a strength/mobility deficit upstream. Common triathlon injuries: patellofemoral pain (runner's knee), ITB syndrome, Achilles tendinopathy, plantar fasciitis, medial tibial stress syndrome (shin splints)/stress fractures, swimmer's shoulder, and cycling-related low-back pain ([Tri Training Harder — 5 common injuries](https://tritrainingharder.com/blog/2020/10/5-common-triathlete-injuries-amp-how-to-avoid-them), [TriathlonHealth](https://triathlonhealth.com/training/injury-prevention/)).

| Injury | Where/what | Typical root cause | Early warning sign |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Runner's knee (PFPS)** | Pain around/under kneecap | Weak glutes/quads, knee valgus, volume spike | Ache going downstairs/downhill, after longer runs |
| **ITB syndrome** | Sharp lateral knee pain | Weak hip abductors (glute med), overstriding, cambered roads | Lateral knee tightness that worsens at a fixed distance |
| **Achilles tendinopathy** | Pain/stiffness in tendon | Calf weakness, rapid mileage/hill/speed increase, low cadence | Morning stiffness, tender nodule, pain at start of run |
| **Plantar fasciitis** | Heel/arch pain | Calf/foot weakness, volume spike, poor footwear | First-steps-in-morning heel pain |
| **Shin splints (MTSS)** | Medial shin pain | Bone/muscle overload, volume spike, overstriding | Diffuse shin ache during/after runs |
| **Stress fracture** | Focal bone pain | Chronic overload, RED-S/low energy availability, volume spike | Pinpoint pain, worse with impact, night pain — RED FLAG |
| **Swimmer's shoulder** | Anterior shoulder impingement | Internal-rotator/adductor overuse vs weak external rotators + scap; high swim volume; poor catch | Ache with overhead reach, front of shoulder during/after swim |
| **Cycling low-back pain** | Lumbar ache on/after rides | Weak core, poor bike fit, too-aggressive aero position, long hours bent over | Progressive low-back ache late in rides |

## 2. Load Management — the #1 Prevention Lever

- **≤10% weekly volume increase per sport** (esp. run), with a deload every 3–4 weeks (methodology §3.1, §7).
- **Acute:Chronic Workload Ratio (ACWR):** keep the 7-day load ÷ 28-day rolling average in the **~0.8–1.3 "sweet spot"; ≥1.5 is the danger zone** for injury ([Science for Sport — ACWR](https://www.scienceforsport.com/acutechronic-workload-ratio/), [systematic review PMC7047972](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7047972/)). This is the quantitative version of "don't spike." Maps to the PMC ramp-rate guardrail (methodology §7.2).
- **Change one variable at a time** (volume OR intensity OR terrain OR shoes), never several — most run injuries follow a compound spike.
- **Cadence:** aim ~170–180 spm running; higher cadence / shorter stride reduces peak load on knees/shins/Achilles ([Triathlete — Achilles](https://www.triathlete.com/training/injury-prevention/4-exercises-to-treat-and-prevent-achilles-tendinitis/)).
- **Surfaces & shoes:** rotate run surfaces, avoid always-cambered roads (ITB), replace shoes ~500–800 km, rotate 2 pairs.
- **Fuel the training:** under-fuelling / low energy availability drives bone stress injuries and poor tissue repair (RED-S — see `nutrition-deep-dive.md` §7). This is a hidden injury cause coaches miss.

## 3. Prehab Routines (concrete, 2–3×/week)

Integrate into strength sessions (`workout-library-session-design.md` §5). "Prevention" = the strength/mobility that closes the upstream deficit.

### 3.1 Run durability circuit (hips, calves, feet) — 15 min, 2–3×/wk
- **Glute med / hip abductor:** side-lying leg raises or banded lateral walks 2×15/side; single-leg glute bridge 2×12/side. (Prevents ITBS + runner's knee — weak abductors let the knee collapse inward, [Tri Training Harder](https://tritrainingharder.com/blog/2020/10/5-common-triathlete-injuries-amp-how-to-avoid-them).)
- **Calf/Achilles:** heavy slow calf raises, straight-knee **and** bent-knee, 3×8–12; progress to single-leg. (Prevents Achilles/plantar; builds tendon capacity.)
- **Single-leg control:** single-leg RDL 2×8/side, step-downs 2×10/side (patellar tracking).
- **Foot/arch:** short-foot exercise, toe yoga, towel scrunches 2×/wk (plantar fascia/shin).

### 3.2 Achilles/plantar rehab-prehab — **Alfredson eccentric protocol**
- **Eccentric calf raises off a step:** rise on both feet, shift to injured leg, **lower over ~3–10 s**, both straight-knee and bent-knee. **3×15 each, twice daily.** Decades of evidence for Achilles tendinopathy ([Marathon Handbook](https://marathonhandbook.com/achilles-tendonitis-exercises/), [Triathlete](https://www.triathlete.com/training/injury-prevention/4-exercises-to-treat-and-prevent-achilles-tendinitis/)). "Some discomfort during is acceptable" (see pain rules §5).

### 3.3 Swimmer's-shoulder prehab — external rotators + scapular control, 2–3×/wk
- **Band external rotation** (elbow at side, and at 90° abduction) 2–3×12–15 — the key weak muscle group ([Active — prevent swimmer's shoulder](https://www.active.com/triathlon/articles/exercises-to-prevent-swimmer-s-shoulder-875223), [220Triathlon](https://www.220triathlon.com/training/injuries/swimmers-shoulder-what-causes-it-and-three-exercises-to-treat-it)).
- **Scapular retraction / YTWs / face-pulls** 2×12 (posture, scap stability).
- **Serratus / scap push-ups**, prone Ys.
- **Technique:** high-elbow catch, avoid crossing midline, don't blast swim volume up; a 12-week program reduces in-season rotational imbalance ([RCT, PMC11899141](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11899141/)).

### 3.4 Cyclist low-back prehab — core + bike fit
- **Anti-extension/rotation core:** plank & side-plank 3×30–45 s, dead-bug 2×10, bird-dog 2×10, Pallof press 2×12. A strong core lets you hold aero without lumbar overload ([220Triathlon — back pain](https://www.220triathlon.com/training/bike-training/how-to-prevent-back-pain-caused-by-cycling)).
- **Bike fit is the biggest single fix:** a personalized, dynamic bike fit reduces low-back pain and is more effective than any exercise if the fit is wrong ([systematic review PMC12907770](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12907770/)). Ease into an aggressive aero position over weeks; hip flexibility/hamstring mobility helps.
- **Anterior shin (cycling):** often a bike-fit/cleat issue (foot position, saddle height) — check fit before assuming a run-type shin splint.

## 4. Early Warning Signs a Coach Watches

- **Niggle that's the same spot every session**, or that warms up then returns.
- **Pain that worsens *within* a run, or takes >24 h to settle** (see §5 — a load-too-high signal).
- **Morning stiffness** in Achilles/plantar; **first-steps heel pain**.
- **Focal/pinpoint bone pain worse with impact, night pain** → possible stress fracture — **stop and refer** (do not "run through").
- **Asymmetry** creeping into gait/form, a new limp, altered cadence.
- **Recurrent minor niggles** across body = whole-body overload / under-recovery (methodology §7; `what-a-coach-actually-does.md` §6).
- **Shoulder ache lingering after swims**; **low-back ache appearing earlier each long ride.**

Coach move: **ask about niggles every check-in** and act early — catching a tendinopathy at "grade 1 morning stiffness" costs a week; ignoring it costs a season.

## 5. Pain Decision Rules (train / modify / stop)

Use the **monitored 0–10 pain scale** (physio-standard; [Sports Injury Physio](https://www.sports-injury-physio.com/post/running-after-injury), [running-physio](https://www.running-physio.com/returnafterinjury/)):

| Pain during activity | Rule |
|---|---|
| **0–2 / 10** (mild awareness) | **Train** — acceptable; monitor |
| **3 / 10** | **Ceiling** — OK to continue but do not progress load; hold or reduce |
| **>3 / 10, or rising during the session** | **Stop / modify now** — likely causing damage |
| **Sharp, focal, or altering your gait** | **Stop** — especially bone pain (stress-fracture caution) |

The three "you did too much" flags (any one = back off next time):
1. Pain **>3/10** during activity.
2. Pain **increases** as you continue.
3. Symptoms take **>24 h** to settle (incl. next-morning stiffness worse than baseline).

**Modify, don't martyr:** if running hurts, cross-train the aerobic load in the pool (aqua-jogging) or on the bike — you can hold fitness while the tissue calms. **Something painless beats nothing; nothing beats making it worse.**

## 6. Return-to-Training After INJURY (walk-run progression)

Gate before running again ([Sports Injury Physio](https://www.sports-injury-physio.com/post/running-after-injury), [running-physio](https://www.running-physio.com/returnafterinjury/)):
- **Entry criteria:** walk 30+ min pain-free with no next-day symptoms, **and pass a load test — e.g. 20 single-leg hops pain-free** on the affected side.

### 6.1 Walk-run progression
- Start **run 1–2 min / walk 1 min × 5–8**, on **alternate days** (rest day between run days).
- **Progress ONLY if pain stayed ≤3/10 during, after, and next morning.** If a session felt hard or flared symptoms, **repeat it** (don't advance) until easy.
- Typical progression over ~4–8 weeks: lengthen run intervals → reduce walk → continuous easy run → then reintroduce volume, *then* (last) intensity.
- Keep all return runs **easy/conversational**; add extra rest days if needed.

### 6.2 Rebuilding after time off (detraining-aware — see also §8)
- Aerobic fitness declines meaningfully after ~10–14 days off; **VO2max drops ~4–14% in trained athletes over 2–4 weeks**, strength lasts a bit longer ([Frontiers detraining review](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/physiology/articles/10.3389/fphys.2023.1334766/full), [TrainingPeaks detraining](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/blog/how-much-down-time-is-too-much-the-concept-of-detr/)). See `plan-adaptation-coaching-logic.md` §5 for the day-count re-plan table.
- **Rebuild rule:** re-enter the plan at a level you can complete comfortably, then progress ~10%/wk. Do not resume at pre-injury volume.

## 7. Return-to-Training After ILLNESS

### 7.1 The neck check (day-to-day decision)
- **Above the neck only** (runny nose, sneezing, mild sore throat, **no fever**): easy/short training usually OK; drop intensity, cut volume, reassess ([Triathlete — dealing with illness](https://www.triathlete.com/training/the-triathletes-guide-to-dealing-with-illness/); methodology/what-a-coach §3.3).
- **Below the neck** (chest congestion, productive cough, body aches, GI) **or ANY fever**: **NO training — full stop.** Training with a fever or systemic illness risks myocarditis and prolongs illness.
- **Fever rule:** no training until **afebrile ≥24 h without fever-reducing meds**, then ease back.

### 7.2 Return after fever/flu/COVID — graduated protocol
Follow a **staged return, each stage ≥1 day, only advancing if symptom-free** ([TrainingPeaks — COVID return-to-play](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/coach-blog/covid-19-return-to-play-protocols/), [return-to-play consensus PMC8530427](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8530427/)):
- **Stage 0:** rest until symptom-free at rest (and ≥24 h afebrile). For COVID, ≥10 days from onset is commonly advised for symptomatic cases.
- **Stage 1:** light activity (walk / very easy spin, ≤ HR 70% max), short.
- **Stage 2:** easy aerobic, low volume; add a second easy session.
- **Stage 3:** reintroduce normal aerobic volume, still no hard intensity.
- **Stage 4:** reintroduce intensity / normal training.
- Or the **50/30/20/10 volume rule** over 4 weeks (cut load ≥50% wk1, then 30/20/10%) for mild infection.
- **If ANY symptom returns** (esp. **excessive fatigue, breathlessness, chest pain, palpitations, resting-HR elevation**): drop back a stage after ≥24 h symptom-free. **Chest pain / palpitations / breathless-out-of-proportion → stop and see a doctor** (cardiac red flags).
- **HRV/RHR are useful gates:** a still-suppressed HRV or elevated resting HR means the body isn't ready — hold the stage (methodology §7.3, §8).

### 7.3 Illness time-off re-plan (mirror of injury)
- **2–5 days lost:** fitness ~intact; ease back, maybe reschedule one key session, no panic.
- **6–14 days:** rewind the progression **1–2 weeks** before the illness and rebuild; take intervals **down a zone** and make recoveries more active on the first sessions back.
- **>14 days:** treat as a mini re-base (see `plan-adaptation-coaching-logic.md` §5).

## If-Then Rules Summary

- IF prescribing run load → THEN cap increases at ≤10%/wk and keep ACWR ~0.8–1.3 (avoid ≥1.5); change only one variable (volume/intensity/terrain/shoes) at a time.
- IF an athlete reports a repeating same-spot niggle → THEN address it early: reduce load, add sport-specific prehab, cross-train the aerobic volume; catching it early saves the season.
- IF pain is 0–2/10 → THEN train; IF 3/10 → THEN hold, don't progress; IF >3/10, rising, or >24 h to settle → THEN stop/modify; IF focal bone pain or gait-altering → THEN stop and refer (stress-fracture caution).
- IF running hurts but cycling/swimming don't → THEN maintain aerobic load via bike/aqua-jog while the tissue calms.
- IF returning from injury → THEN gate on pain-free 30-min walk + 20 single-leg hops, then run/walk on alternate days, progressing only when pain stayed ≤3/10 through next morning; rebuild volume before intensity.
- IF symptoms are above the neck, no fever → THEN easy/short training OK; IF below the neck or ANY fever → THEN full rest, no training until afebrile ≥24 h.
- IF returning after fever/flu/COVID → THEN staged return (each stage ≥1 day, advance only if symptom-free) or 50/30/20/10 over 4 weeks; drop back a stage if symptoms recur.
- IF chest pain, palpitations, or disproportionate breathlessness post-illness → THEN stop and seek medical review (cardiac red flag).
- IF >5 days lost to illness/injury → THEN rewind the progression 1–2 weeks and take early intervals down a zone; >14 days → mini re-base.
- IF recurrent niggles across multiple sites, or bone/tissue not recovering → THEN suspect under-recovery / low energy availability (RED-S) and audit fueling and sleep.
- IF low-back pain on the bike → THEN prioritize a professional bike fit + core prehab before adding training.
