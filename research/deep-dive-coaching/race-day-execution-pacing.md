# Race-Day Execution & Pacing — First-Timer Full-Distance Playbook

**Purpose:** The methodology file establishes training zones, FTP/CSS, and race power ranges. This file is the **execution playbook**: discipline-by-discipline pacing for a first-time full-distance athlete (swim, bike by power/HR, run with walk-run and aid-station strategy), **heat/weather adaptation and adjustments**, and the **common first-timer mistakes** coaches actively prevent. The Ironman is won or lost on execution, not fitness — a fit athlete who mispaces will walk the marathon.

> Evidence note: Pacing numbers (IF ranges, run-walk splits) come from coaching practice and power-data experience, not RCTs; they're well-established convention. Heat acclimation rests on decent physiology evidence. Flagged where extrapolated.

---

## 1. Swim (3.8 km) — Survive It, Don't Race It

For a beginner-to-intermediate, the swim is about **arriving on the bike calm and unfatigued**, not saving minutes.

- **Pace easy/controlled** — aim near CSS or a touch below (methodology §3.3); the swim is a tiny fraction of total time and over-swimming costs the whole day.
- **Beat the mass-start panic:** start at the **side/back**, sight regularly, settle breathing early. Open-water start anxiety is a top first-timer failure point — rehearse it (imagery + open-water practice, see mental file §2).
- **Draft legally** off feet/hips to save energy if comfortable.
- **Don't sprint the first 200 m** — the single most common swim error; it triggers panic and early fatigue. Start controlled, build slightly.
- **Exit calm:** walk-not-sprint through T1, get HR down, start fueling.

**AI-coach implication:** set a "controlled, at/below CSS, don't sprint the start" swim goal; drill open-water skills and start position in prep; include swim-start imagery in the mental toolkit.

## 2. Bike (180 km) — Where the Race Is Decided

The bike is the largest time block and the discipline where pacing discipline most directly protects the marathon. **Ride too hard here and you walk the run** — that costs 20–90 minutes, far more than any bike time saved.

### 2.1 Power-based pacing (preferred)

Anchor on **Intensity Factor (IF) = Normalized Power ÷ FTP** — dimensionless, so it's comparable regardless of your FTP ([Roadman — IM wattage](https://roadmancycling.com/blog/what-wattage-should-you-ride-in-an-ironman), [Fitwerx — power meter pacing](https://fitwerx.com/how-to-use-a-power-meter-for-optimal-ironman-pacing/)):

| Athlete | Target IF (% of FTP as NP) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **First-time finisher** | **0.65–0.70** | Deliberately conservative; protects the run |
| **Experienced age-grouper** | **0.68–0.78** | Toward top only on flat, steady courses |
| **Danger zone** | **>0.80** | "Gambling with the marathon" — avoid |
| **Sandbagging** | **<0.65** | Leaving too much on the table (rare problem for first-timers) |

- **Target watts = FTP × target IF.** (FTP 250 W × 0.70 = 175 W target NP.) This aligns with the methodology file's "IM race power ≈ 68–78% FTP" (§3.2), with first-timers at the low end.
- **Keep Variability Index low** (ride steady, ~VI < 1.05): surging on hills and coasting descents wastes matches. Cap power on climbs (don't chase); recover on descents. On hilly courses, ride a slightly **lower average IF** to control VI ([Roadman](https://roadmancycling.com/blog/what-wattage-should-you-ride-in-an-ironman), [TrainingPeaks — Kona bike](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/blog/lessons-from-kona-how-to-nail-an-ironman-bike/)).
- **First ~30–60 min: hold back deliberately** — adrenaline makes goal power feel too easy; the discipline is riding *under* what feels right early.

### 2.2 HR-based pacing (if no power meter, or as a check)

- Ride in **low aerobic HR** (roughly upper Z2, methodology §3.2). **HR drifts up** over hours at constant effort (cardiac drift) — expect it and don't chase a fixed number late; if HR climbs while power/effort is steady, that's normal, but if it's climbing *and* you feel cooked, ease off.
- Combine: power sets the ceiling, HR/RPE confirm you're not overcooking, especially in heat.

### 2.3 On-bike fueling & the run hand-off

- Execute the rehearsed fuel/fluid/sodium plan (methodology §9, nutrition file) — the bike is where you **bank the carbs and fluid** the run depends on; you can eat far more on the bike than the run.
- **Ease the final ~15–20 min** slightly to prime the legs for T2.

**AI-coach implication:** generate a course-specific bike plan — target NP/IF (0.65–0.70 for a first-timer), a hard "don't exceed" ceiling, climb power caps, a "hold back for 45 min" reminder, and the fuel schedule. If no power meter, give an HR ceiling + RPE and warn about cardiac drift.

## 3. Run (42.2 km) — Execute the Plan You Made Before the Race

### 3.1 Pacing reality for first-timers

- **Expect the IM marathon to be ~20–30 min slower than your standalone marathon** even on a great day, due to cumulative fatigue ([NVDM Coaching](https://www.nvdmcoaching.com/post/ironman-marathon-pacing-strategy)).
- **Start conservatively** — the #1 run error is going out too fast (e.g., 8:00/mi when you'll finish far slower), which guarantees a blow-up. Start **easier than feels right**; the first few km should feel almost too easy.
- **Negative-split intent:** the back **8 miles/13 km is where you overtake people** — aim to have enough left to run it strong; that's a huge time and morale swing ([Triathlon Swim Squad — the IM run](https://triathlonswimsquad.com/the-ironman-run/)).

### 3.2 Run-walk & aid-station strategy (evidence-informed)

Strategic walking is **not** failure — it's a performance tactic:

- **Walk the aid stations** (a brief ~30–60 s walk) at least through ~mile 18 / ~km 29. This is biomechanically near-neutral, lets you actually drink/fuel, drops core temperature, and gives the calves a micro-break. Athletes who do short aid-station walks often **run the back half faster** than those who refuse to walk ([CoachRay — walk aid stations](https://www.coachray.nz/2026/06/23/why-walking-aid-stations-can-make-you-faster-at-ironman/), [NVDM](https://www.nvdmcoaching.com/post/ironman-marathon-pacing-strategy)).
- **Cost/benefit:** a ~45 s walk per aid station costs ~45 s each; **falling apart at mile 20 costs 20–40 min.** The math overwhelmingly favors the disciplined walk breaks.
- **Structured run-walk** (e.g., run to each aid station, walk through it with good upright posture, then resume; or Galloway-style intervals for less-trained runners — methodology §3.1) extends durability and is fully race-legal ([Tri Training Harder — run-walk strategy](https://tritrainingharder.com/blog/2020/3/putting-together-your-run-walk-strategy-for-a-marathon-or-ironman-marathon)).
- **Decide the walk/run rule before the race** and rehearse it in long runs/bricks — a fatigued brain shouldn't improvise it (see mental file §1).

### 3.3 Aid-station execution — what to grab and when

- Have a **standing order** so you're not deciding while cooked: e.g., every station — sip fuel/electrolyte, alternate water; take cola/gels from a set point (often the back half) when solid food is unappealing.
- **Cooling in heat:** grab ice (down the suit, in the hands, under the cap), sponges, and water over the head/neck at every station (see §4).
- **Fuel to plan, but flexible:** if the gut rebels, switch to cola/water and simpler carbs rather than forcing the plan — GI shutdown is common late.

**AI-coach implication:** produce a printable/lockscreen run plan: starting pace (deliberately easy), the run-walk rule, per-aid-station checklist (fuel/fluid/cooling), the "hold back early, attack the last 13 km" strategy, and a "if gut fails → cola + water" fallback.

## 4. Heat, Weather & Environment

Heat is the biggest environmental performance-killer and a first-timer blindspot.

### 4.1 Heat acclimation (pre-race)

- **Timeline:** meaningful adaptation in **10–14 days** of heat exposure; ~10 consecutive days of ~90 min in the heat gives strong acclimation ([RunnersConnect — heat](https://runnersconnect.net/heat-acclimatization/), [Precision — heat training guide](https://www.precisionhydration.com/performance-advice/performance/heat-training-guide-for-endurance-athletes/)).
- **Adaptations:** earlier/greater sweating, more dilute sweat (less sodium loss), lower core temp and HR at a given effort, plasma volume expansion — worth **~5–10%+** reduction in heat-related performance loss ([Simple Endurance — heat acclimation](https://simpleendurance.medium.com/mastering-heat-acclimation-strategies-for-endurance-athletes-in-cycling-running-and-triathlon-5c8709076620)).
- **Methods:** train in the heat, or use **passive heat** (sauna or hot bath post-session) — mixed active+passive works well and is gentler on taper load; e.g., 5 active + 3 passive sessions over 2 weeks fully adapted international triathletes ([PMC — post-exercise hot-water immersion](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6305481/)).
- **Decay is fast (~2.5%/day):** finish acclimation **close to race** (a block ~2 weeks out, topped up in race week) — don't do it months early and let it fade ([RunnersConnect](https://runnersconnect.net/heat-acclimatization/)).

### 4.2 Racing in heat (execution)

- **Pace down:** target the **lower end** of bike IF and slow run pace; heat raises cardiovascular strain — HR at goal power will be higher.
- **Hydrate/sodium up:** higher sweat and sodium losses; lean on the personalized sweat/sodium plan (nutrition file §4); pre-cool if possible.
- **Aggressive active cooling** on the run: ice in suit/hands/cap, sponges, water over head at every aid station.
- **Watch for heat illness:** stop for dizziness, chills/goosebumps in heat, confusion, nausea, cessation of sweating — these are medical, not "push through" moments.

### 4.3 Cold / wind / rain

- **Cold:** arm warmers, gloves, extra layer in T1 for the bike; risk is under-fueling because you don't feel thirsty — keep drinking.
- **Wind:** ride by **power, not speed** (speed is meaningless in wind); expect slower times, don't panic-chase.
- **Wet:** caution on descents/corners; protect nutrition from washout.

**AI-coach implication:** if the target race is historically hot, schedule a heat-acclimation block ~2 weeks out (active + optional sauna/hot-bath) and a race-week top-up; on a hot forecast, auto-lower pacing targets, raise fluid/sodium, add a cooling checklist, and include heat-illness stop-signs.

## 5. Common First-Timer Mistakes (and How Coaches Prevent Them)

| Mistake | Consequence | Prevention |
|---|---|---|
| **Sprinting the swim start** | Panic, early fatigue | Controlled start, side/back, breathing drill, open-water + imagery rehearsal |
| **Riding the bike too hard (IF too high)** | Walked/blown marathon (−20–90 min) | Hard IF ceiling (0.65–0.70), "hold back 45 min," power cap on climbs |
| **Starting the run too fast** | Blow-up ~km 30 | Deliberately-easy first km, negative-split plan |
| **Refusing to walk aid stations** | Slower back half, missed fueling | Pre-set walk-the-aid-station rule through ~mile 18 |
| **Under-fueling / trying new nutrition on race day** | Bonk, GI disaster | Rehearsed plan only; nothing new; bank carbs on bike |
| **Poor hydration/sodium in heat** | Cramps, heat illness, DNF | Personalized sweat/sodium plan; cooling routine |
| **No pre-decided pacing/nutrition/logistics plan** | Bad in-race decisions when cooked | Written race plan + rehearsed in training/bricks |
| **Chasing others / ego pacing early** | Everyone else's race, not yours | Process focus; race your numbers, not the crowd |
| **T1/T2 chaos** | Lost time, forgotten gear/fuel | Rehearsed transition sequence, gear checklist |
| **Ignoring the taper / arriving tired** | Flat legs | Follow taper (methodology §10) |

Overarching theme: **the plan is made before race day and rehearsed in training** (long bricks that mirror the race — methodology §4). Race day is execution of a rehearsed plan, not improvisation ([Alpha State — IM race strategies](https://alphastatecoaching.com/news/Ironman-Race-Strategies)).

**AI-coach implication:** generate a single consolidated **race-execution document** in race week (swim/bike/run targets, fueling/hydration schedule, aid-station and cooling checklists, walk-run rule, transition sequences, mental toolkit, weather adjustments, heat stop-signs), and drill its components in the peak-block bricks so nothing on race day is new.

## 6. Practitioner Execution Insights (from expert podcasts/videos)

The transcripts in [`youtube-transcripts/`](youtube-transcripts/) provide a detailed age-group case study plus pro execution detail that corroborate this playbook.

**Age-group case study — Norman Banick, top-15 at the IM World Championship (backs §2, §3, §4).** A near-textbook execution of everything above:
- **Bike = strict power discipline, not speed** (§2.1): normalized power 235 W / average 226 W over 4:49, ~10% harder into headwinds and up climbs, backing off dangerous crosswind descents. Time-in-zone analysis: ~77% in Z2–Z3, only ~5% at sweet spot or above. When his legs blew ~230 km, he consciously *dropped* power to a sustainable level rather than forcing target — exactly §2.1's "don't chase" discipline ([Banick — IM Worlds](youtube-transcripts/trainerroad-norman-banick-ironman-worlds-age-grouper.md)).
- **Rehearsed, self-mixed fuel** (§2.3, nutrition file): ~90 g carbs/h (2:1 glucose:fructose) on the bike, dropped to ~50–60 g/h on the run — tested across prior races. He still ran out of energy late and had to walk sections, underlining how hard fueling is to sustain in Kona heat.
- **Heat execution** (§4.2): stopped ~30 s at *every* aid station to load ice into sleeves/suit and douse with water — matching §4.2's aggressive-cooling instruction.
- **Nearly-free heat prep** (§4.1): a hot bath immediately after workouts a few times per week left him markedly more heat-tolerant than a prior year at similar conditions — a concrete, low-cost version of §4.1's passive-heat method. He also advises arriving 7+ days early (jet lag + heat) and reconning the course for wind.
- **Biggest year-over-year gain** wasn't equipment but simply becoming a better runner (consistent mileage + real intervals) plus better heat management — his run split dropped from 3:30 to 3:15.

**Pro race-day fueling logistics (extends §2.3, §3.3).** Pros pre-plan intake in thirds, stash fuel at special-needs / pro stations to avoid carrying weight, secure bottle cages with elastic bands after losing bottles on rough courses, and reach for fuel only during easy/slow sections rather than at 45–50 km/h ([Chevalier — carrying race fuel](youtube-transcripts/gtn-leon-chevalier-how-pros-carry-race-fuel.md)). Their plans include explicit first-hour targets and pre-planned heat/wind adjustments ([what pros eat race day](youtube-transcripts/gtn-what-pro-triathletes-eat-race-day-ironman.md)).

**Advanced pacing & core-temp detail (extends §2, §4.2).** Iden/Blummenfelt treat the bike as a "bank account" — know your upper-limit numbers and the cost of each withdrawal (Iden's decisive Kona moves included a ~370 W, 10-min surge to bridge). On heat, they used a CORE body-temp sensor and learned you feel temperature *change*, not absolute temperature — rising power + rising core temp at steady pace signals the coming wall, a useful refinement to §4.2's "watch for heat illness" ([Norwegian method — Rich Roll](youtube-transcripts/rich-roll-iden-blummenfelt-norwegian-method.md)). These are elite tactics — for a first-timer the conservative, steady-pacing defaults in §2–§3 remain the priority.

## Race-Day Rules Summary (If-Then)

- IF swimming → THEN controlled, at/below CSS, side/back start, don't sprint the first 200 m, exit calm.
- IF pacing the bike (first-timer) → THEN NP/IF **0.65–0.70** (target watts = FTP × IF), steady (low VI), cap climb power, hold back the first ~45 min, execute fuel plan.
- IF no power meter → THEN ride upper-Z2 HR + RPE, expect cardiac drift, don't chase a fixed HR late.
- IF running → THEN start deliberately easy, plan a negative split, **walk aid stations ~30–60 s through mile 18**, execute per-station fuel/fluid/cooling, keep a "gut fails → cola + water" fallback.
- IF the race is hot → THEN acclimate ~2 wk out (active ± sauna, top up race week), lower pacing targets, raise fluid/sodium, cool aggressively (ice/sponges every station), and stop for heat-illness signs.
- IF cold/windy/wet → THEN layer + keep fueling (cold), ride by power not speed (wind), descend cautiously (wet).
- IF race week → THEN deliver one consolidated, rehearsed race-execution plan; nothing on race day should be new.

*Compiled July 2026. Pacing conventions are coaching/power-data practice; heat acclimation rests on decent physiology evidence. Individualize to the athlete, course, and forecast.*
